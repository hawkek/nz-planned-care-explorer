# Three findings (week 3 test)

The plan asks for three plain-English findings, to test whether the analysis
says something. These use the provisional OIA data to December 2025 and the
site's neutral wording. Check them again after the first live data update and
the Health NZ definitions review, before using them in any launch material.

1. **Seven times as many people waiting over four months for a first
   appointment as in 2019.** In January 2019, about 10,250 patients had waited
   more than four months for a first specialist assessment. In December 2025
   the figure was about 75,200. That is 37.8% of everyone waiting for a first
   assessment, up from 8.6%. The highest month was about 83,000 in early 2025.
   (The released figure for January 2025, about 86,000, includes an apparent
   double count in Hawke's Bay; see `docs/data_exploration.md`.) Over the last
   24 months the national number shows no clear rise or fall. As a cross-check,
   Health NZ's own health target result for June 2025 (62.0% waiting under four
   months) matches this data exactly.

2. **Waits for promised treatment were lower at the end of 2025 than a year
   earlier.** About 27,700 patients were waiting more than four months after
   being given a commitment to treatment in December 2025. That is about 5,300
   fewer than in December 2024, but still about six times the January 2019
   figure of about 4,400.

3. **Districts are moving in different directions.** Over the last 24 months,
   the number waiting more than four months for a first specialist assessment
   rose clearly in 10 districts (Canterbury, Capital and Coast, Hutt Valley,
   MidCentral, Nelson Marlborough, Northland, South Canterbury, Southern,
   Wairarapa and West Coast). It fell clearly in 6 (Bay of Plenty, Counties
   Manukau, Tairawhiti, Taranaki, Waikato and Waitemata), with no clear trend
   in the other 4. Even in the districts where it is falling fastest, the
   trend would take more than 10 years to reach the goal of no one waiting
   more than four months, if it continued unchanged.

Caveats to state alongside any of these: the counts include only patients
accepted onto a list; the data is provisional; and it is not yet confirmed
whether patients are counted by the district that treats them or the district
where they live.
